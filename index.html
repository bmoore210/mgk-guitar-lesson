How to practice this

  1. Capo on fret 1. All the diagrams count frets from the capo, so “C” is your normal open C.
  2. Learn Em first (2 fingers, easiest), then C, then D. Tap each in the library to hear what it should sound like.
  3. Drill the changes, hardest first — 1 minute each, count how many clean switches you get:
    • D → C (hardest: every finger moves; it happens 35 times). Trick: move your ring finger to the A string first — it’s the bass note you strum first.
    • Em → D (quick: Em only lasts 2 beats). Lift early on the last “&” — a bit of open‑string noise is invisible under the band.
    • C → Em (easiest): lift your index finger, middle and ring each drop one string.
  4. Play‑along mode at 60–70 BPM with the “4 downs” strum. When you can do 4 loops without stopping, go 8 downs, then raise the tempo 10 BPM at a time.
  5. Your record at 0.75×, then 0.85×, then 1×. Use A–B loop on the chorus (0:47–1:02) until it’s automatic.
  6. Watch for the two surprises: the 2‑beat stop at 0:30 (“N.C.” — mute the strings with your palm) and the last chord, which is Em, not D.
  7. Too hard right now? Use the easy shapes (Cmaj7, one‑finger Em, Dsus2) in the library — they sound fine in this song.
